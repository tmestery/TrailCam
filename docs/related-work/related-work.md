# Related Work

## 1. The Perceptron: A Probabilistic Model for Information Storage and Organization in the Brain

**Author:** Frank Rosenblatt (1958)

**Description:**  
This paper introduces the perceptron, one of the foundational linear classification algorithms. It provides a simple baseline for comparing more complex models in our wildlife species classification task.

**Links:**  
- [Paper — PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13602029/)
- [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0042519)

---

## 2. The Regression Analysis of Binary Sequences

**Author:** D. R. Cox (1958)

**Description:**  
This paper presents an early foundation for regression methods involving binary outcomes. It is relevant to our logistic regression model, which will provide a simple statistical baseline for species classification.

**Links:**  
- [Paper — Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/20/2/215/7027376)
- [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1958.tb00292.x)

---

## 3. Support-Vector Networks

**Authors:** Corinna Cortes and Vladimir Vapnik (1995)

**Description:**  
This paper introduces support-vector networks, the foundation of Support Vector Machines. SVMs are useful for classification in high-dimensional feature spaces and will be used to classify wildlife species from engineered visual features.

**Links:**  
- [Paper — Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00994018)
- [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00994018)

---

## 4. Gradient-Based Learning Applied to Document Recognition

**Authors:** Yann LeCun, Léon Bottou, Yoshua Bengio, and Patrick Haffner (1998)

**Description:**  
This paper demonstrates the use of convolutional neural networks for image recognition. It provides foundational work for our CNN, which will learn visual features directly from animal images.

**Links:**  
- [Paper — IEEE](https://doi.org/10.1109/5.726791)
- [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/5.726791)

---

## 5. Machine Learning to Classify Animal Species in Camera Trap Images: Applications in Ecology

**Authors:** Michael A. Tabak, Mohammad S. Norouzzadeh, David W. Wolfson, et al. (2019)

**Description:**  
This paper applies convolutional neural networks to automatically classify wildlife species in camera-trap images. It is especially relevant to our project because it demonstrates the practical use of machine learning for large-scale camera-trap wildlife monitoring.

**Links:**  
- [Paper — Wiley](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/2041-210x.13120)
- [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.13120)
